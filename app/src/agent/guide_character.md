# Building, dressing and arming a 3D figure

For the skills agent: this is the short how-to for the `model` character, the `wear` op and the `hold` op. It works for any figure (a person of any age, a stylised or cartoon character, a rigged model of your own) and any clothes or props. Read it with `guide {"topic": "wardrobe"}`.

**1. Build the body.** `{"op": "model", "shape": "character", "size": 400, "dressed": false, ...}`. Start from a `preset` (baby, toddler, child, teen, adult, elder, slim, athletic, heavy, heroic, realistic, stylised, toon, chibi, anime, anime_chibi) and change anything: `age` (years or a word), `style` (realistic, stylised, toon, anime, or 0 to 1; `anime` gives a bigger head, long legs, big eyes with a large iris and highlights, a small nose and mouth, flat colour, hard-shaded hair, and the clip is drawn toon-shaded), `figure` (-1 broad to 1 narrow-shouldered, full-hipped; or masculine, feminine), `build`, `shoulders`, `hips`, `legs`, `arms`, `neck`, `hands`, `head_size`, `skin`. Every figure is born with sculpted hair that is its own, not the outfit's (`dressed: false` and `undress` keep it): `hair` is its colour (and the brows'), a style word, `false` for none, or `{"style", "colour", "strands": true, "length"}`; `hairstyle` names the style (short, buzz, crew, pixie, bob, long, wavy, curly, afro, ponytail, bun, spiky, twintails, bangs). The body and every garment are closed solids (no open edges for `check`). `species` (cat, dog, fox, bear, rabbit, mouse; `fur` colour) makes an animal on two legs: ears, muzzle, nose, whiskers and a tail on bones `tail.1`…`tail.3`, with the same body, face and bones, so it is dressed and acted the same way. Use `dressed: false` when you will `wear` clothes, so no plain top or trousers are painted underneath. Every figure has the same bones: `hips spine chest neck head eye.L eye.R`, per side `shoulder upper_arm forearm hand`, a `grip.L/R` control bone at the middle of the fist, five digits of three bones (`thumb.L.0` … `pinky.R.2`), `thigh shin foot toe`. The body is always whole and bare underneath; a figure born `dressed` wears a T-shirt, trousers and shoes as separate named parts (`T-shirt`, `Trousers`, `Shoes`) that `wear` `remove` or replaces. Each finger and the thumb is its own surface on its own three bones. The face is made of separate parts: eyeballs with a clear wet `Cornea` (pupil size by the keys `pupilDilate`, `pupilConstrict`), upper and lower lids with `Lashes` (keys `eyeBlinkLeft/Right`, `eyeSquintLeft/Right`, `eyeWideLeft/Right`; the lids half follow the eyes' gaze), sculpted `Lips` with corners round a real mouth with `Teeth`, `Gums` and a `Tongue` (keys `tongueOut`, `tongueUp`, `tongueDown`, `tongueCurl`, `tongueTH`; bones `jaw`, `tongue.0`, `tongue.1`, `tongue.2`), brows, and the ARKit and viseme shape keys (`jawOpen`, `viseme_aa`, ...). The `jaw` bone and the `jawOpen` key move the same parts: use one of them. The neck is skinned head, neck, chest smoothly. `describe` lists the parts (`parts`, `worn`).

**2. Dress it.** `{"op": "wear", "clip": 3, "items": [{"kind": "coat", "style": "trench", "colour": "#b49a6a", "material": "cotton"}, {"kind": "hat", "style": "fedora"}]}`. Kinds: shirt, jacket, coat, trousers, skirt, dress, cape, hat, shoes, gloves, glasses, scarf, belt, bag, armour. Other fields: `colour2` (trim), `material` (wool, tweed, denim, silk, leather, steel ...), `pattern` (stripes, pinstripe, check, plaid, dots), `fit` (tight … baggy or a number), `length` (waist, hip, thigh, knee, calf, ankle, floor), `sleeves`, `open`, `scale` (hats, bags), `skin` (smooth, rigid, stiff), `detail`, `loose: true` (leaves coat tails, a skirt or a cape to the cloth simulation, on the figure). Clothes are real meshes cut to the body, lifted off it by their ease and skinned from the body's own weights, so they follow every pose and never need a rig. Hard things (hats, glasses, buckles, buttons, armour plates) ride rigid on one bone. Hair is worn too: `{"kind": "hair", "style": "long", "colour": "#5a3a22"}` (short, buzz, crew, pixie, bob, long, wavy, curly, afro, ponytail, bun, spiky, twintails, bangs): sculpted as one closed mass with locks (`"anime": true` cuts them hard for toon shading), or `"strands": true` for groomed hair cards; `beard` (goatee, stubble), `moustache`, `brows` are strands. Hair is skinned to the head (long hair's ends give to the neck and chest), and a hat presses it down under the hat line instead of being pierced by it. Patterns are woven at their real size (a check about 1.5% of the height, a tartan sett 9%) on panel-cut UVs; tight clothes (`fit: "tight"`, leggings) draw the skin in under them. `{"op": "wear", "clip": 3, "list": true}` says what is worn; `remove: "hat"` or `undress: true` takes things off. Wearing the same name again replaces it.

**3. Put things in its hands.** `{"op": "hold", "clip": 3, "hand": "right", "item": {"kit": "cup", "colour": "#cc3333"}}`. The kit: stick (pen, wand, cigarette, with a `tip` colour), cup, glass, bottle, phone, book, pistol, sword, torch, lantern, candle, lighter, briefcase, handbag, umbrella, ball, tray; or `"item": 12` to hold any model clip. `grip` is handle (the default: the fist closes round it), pinch (pen-like) or palm; `turn` [x, y, z] degrees and `offset` [x, y, z] px adjust it; `size` is px. The fingers close by how thick the thing is. `{"release": true, "at": 4}` lets go. A flame is a general fire element: candle, torch and lantern burn by default; any prop can burn with `"flame": {"at": [x, y, z] in the prop's own frame (y down), "size": px}`. A lit flame adds emissive flame layers and a flickering point light that follows the hand (`light: false` for none).

**4. Rigs and edits.** Bone names match in any case and spelling (`upper_arm.l`, `UpperArm.L`, `left_arm`); an unknown name lists the real ones. A `mesh` edit on a rigged model never damages the rig: an added part is skinned rigid on the nearest bone (`bind`: smooth, piece, or bone with `bone`). `rig` `skinning`: `rigid` + `bone` + `material`/`region` binds a hat or prop whole to a bone, `parts` binds each separate piece, `smooth` re-binds; `constraint` `{"kind": "attach", "bone": "head", "material": "Hat"}` does the same for a named material.

**What to check.** Close-up on the face and hands in good light; the coat at a walk stride; the hat turning with the head; the held thing moving with the hand.

**Toon and anime look.** `{"op": "shading", "clips": [3], "shading": "toon", "bands": 2, "outline": 2.5, "outline_colour": "#1a1418", "shadow_colour": "#8c80b0", "rim": 0.35}` draws model clips cel-shaded: the light in 2 or 3 hard bands, the shadow side in its own colour, a rim light, hard highlights on glossy parts and ink outlines round the silhouette (`outline` px, `false` for none). Without `clips` it shades every model clip in the sequence (a scene-wide look); `"shading": "physical"` turns it off. The same fields work on `model` itself, and a character with `style: "anime"` or `"toon"` gets the toon look by default. World layers take `shading` too (cel bands and rim on the GPU).
